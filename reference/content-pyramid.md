# Content Pyramid — repurposing map

One anchor piece becomes many derivative pieces (Gary Vaynerchuk's content-pyramid model, as used in Alex Lieberman's machine). The anchor carries the full argument; derivatives carry one shard each into a format-native container.

## The map

```
                    ANCHOR
        (essay / newsletter / long LinkedIn / video script)
                       │
     ┌────────┬────────┼────────┬────────────┐
  short     thread   long      quote-      hook-only
  tweets            LinkedIn    post        variants
  (1 shard  (anchor  (second   (react to   (A/B test
   each)    compressed) angle)  the source)  openers)
```

## Rules

1. **One shard per derivative.** A tweet gets one claim from the anchor, not a summary of all of them. Summaries are how derivatives become slop.
2. **Match the author's format-native style.** Before writing a derivative, study how the author has historically written in that format (their `voice-guide.md` format sections, or their recent posts in that format). A tweet is not a shrunk LinkedIn post.
3. **Words still come from the interview transcript + anchor.** Repurposing rearranges; it does not invent.
4. **Every derivative passes the Writers Council** at the same ≥ 9.0 gate. Short pieces fail the Allergist *more* often, not less — compression concentrates tells.
5. **Ask before fanning out.** The machine asks which derivatives to produce ("three short tweets, two long LinkedIn posts") rather than generating the full pyramid unprompted.

## Format notes

| Format | Length target | What survives from the anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Short tweet | ≤ 280 chars | The single most falsifiable claim, or the best number |
| Thread (≤ 4 tweets) | 3–4 beats | The anchor's spine: setup → tension → payoff, one beat per tweet |
| Long LinkedIn | 150–300 words above the fold | A *second angle* on the anchor — not the anchor compressed |
| Quote-post / reply | 1–2 sentences | Your position stated against the quoted source |
| Hook variants | 1 line × 3–5 | Alternative openers for testing; body unchanged |

## Anchored example

Anchor claim: *"A banker rebuilt McKinsey's 6-month proposal in a week with Claude Code."*

❌ **Bad derivative (summary-shaped, invented framing):**
> "AI is disrupting consulting! Here are 5 takeaways from a fascinating story I heard this week... 🧵"

Summarizes instead of sharding, adds hype vocabulary, announces a thread instead of starting one.

✅ **Good derivative (one shard, transcript words):**
> "McKinsey quoted a bank 6 months. A guy on their team rebuilt it in a week with Claude Code. The pyramid isn't getting disrupted — it's getting repriced."
